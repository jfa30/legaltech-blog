# Design System Strategy: The Editorial Authority

## 1. Overview & Creative North Star
**Creative North Star: "The Modern Magistrate"**

This design system rejects the clinical, "boxy" nature of standard legal tech in favor of a high-end editorial experience. It balances the legacy and gravitas of *The New York Times* with the kinetic, forward-leaning energy of *The Verge*. 

The system moves beyond a standard blog layout by employing **Intentional Asymmetry**. We utilize generous white space and off-grid element placement to create an experience that feels curated by a human hand, not generated by a template. By layering surfaces rather than boxing them in, we establish a sense of "digital vellum"—sophisticated, deep, and authoritative.

---

## 2. Colors
Our palette is rooted in tradition but executed with modern digital depth.

### Core Roles
*   **Primary (`#022448`)**: Represents the weight of the law. Used for key headlines and brand-level interactions.
*   **Primary Container (`#1e3a5f`)**: Used for immersive sections and deep-mode backgrounds to maintain the "Navy" brand identity.
*   **Tertiary/Gold (`#d4af37`)**: Used sparingly as a "high-performance" accent. It signals premium insights, "Pro" tags, or critical call-outs.
*   **Surface Tiers**: We use the `surface-container` scale (`lowest` to `highest`) to create a hierarchy of information without relying on heavy visual anchors.

### The "No-Line" Rule
**Explicit Instruction:** Prohibit 1px solid borders for sectioning or containment. Boundaries must be defined solely through background color shifts. To separate a featured article from a sidebar, place a `surface-container-lowest` card atop a `surface-container-low` background. 

### Glass & Texture
For floating navigation or sticky headers, utilize **Glassmorphism**. Apply the `surface` color at 80% opacity with a `backdrop-blur` of 12px. This ensures the editorial content "bleeds" through the UI, making the experience feel integrated and fluid rather than fragmented.

---

## 3. Typography
Typography is the voice of this system. We pair the intellectual weight of a serif with the functional clarity of a sans-serif.

*   **Display & Headlines (Newsreader)**: Our serif choice. It should feel "inked." Use `display-lg` (3.5rem) for hero editorial pieces to demand attention. The tight letter-spacing and elegant serifs convey a sense of peer-reviewed trust.
*   **Body & Titles (Inter)**: Our sans-serif workhorse. Used for high-density information and navigation. Inter provides the "tech-focused" edge, ensuring that even complex legal analysis remains readable and modern.
*   **The Contrast Rule**: Always pair a `headline-sm` serif with a `label-md` sans-serif (in all caps) for metadata (e.g., "5 MIN READ"). This juxtaposition is the hallmark of premium editorial design.

---

## 4. Elevation & Depth
We define hierarchy through **Tonal Layering**, mimicking the way physical paper stacks on a desk.

*   **The Layering Principle**: 
    *   **Base:** `surface` (#f8f9fa)
    *   **Content Areas:** `surface-container-low` (#f3f4f5)
    *   **Floating Cards:** `surface-container-lowest` (#ffffff)
*   **Ambient Shadows**: Shadows are strictly prohibited from appearing "gray." If a lift is required (e.g., a hover state on a card), use a diffused shadow: `box-shadow: 0 20px 40px rgba(2, 36, 72, 0.05)`. The tint is derived from the `on-surface` color to mimic natural light.
*   **The Ghost Border Fallback**: If a UI element (like a search input) requires a container on a white background, use the `outline-variant` token at **15% opacity**. It should be felt, not seen.

---

## 5. Components

### Cards & Lists
*   **No Dividers**: Forbid 1px horizontal lines. Use `spacing-8` (2.75rem) to separate list items or subtle background shifts.
*   **The "Verge" Card**: A `surface-container-lowest` card with no border, a subtle ambient shadow on hover, and a `tertiary` (Gold) accent bar (2px) only on the top-left corner to denote "Premium" content.

### Buttons
*   **Primary**: `primary` (#022448) background with `on-primary` (#ffffff) text. Shape: `md` (0.375rem) roundedness. 
*   **Secondary**: `outline` token for text, no background. Use the "Ghost Border" at 20% opacity for the container.
*   **Action**: Use the `tertiary` (Gold) color only for the most critical conversion point (e.g., "Subscribe to Pro").

### Inputs & Forms
*   **Fields**: Use `surface-container-high`. When focused, the background should shift to `surface-container-lowest` with a 2px `primary` bottom-border only. This mimics high-end stationery.

### Sticky Navigation
*   **The "Editorial Header"**: A thin `surface` bar with 85% opacity and backdrop-blur. The logo sits on the left, but the primary navigation is centered to replicate a newspaper masthead.

## 6. Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
*   **Use Asymmetric Spacing**: Place a featured image spanning 8 columns and the text spanning 4 columns to break the "standard blog" feel.
*   **Embrace the "White Space"**: Use `spacing-16` or `20` between major sections. If it feels like too much room, it’s probably just right for a premium audience.
*   **Color-Coded Analysis**: Use the `primary-container` (#1e3a5f) for "Deep Dive" long-form articles to visually signal a shift from news to analysis.

### Don't:
*   **Don't use 100% Black**: Use `on-surface` (#191c1d) for text. It’s softer on the eyes and feels more "printed."
*   **Don't use Gradients**: The depth should come from the `surface-container` tiers, not from color ramps.
*   **Don't use Standard Shadows**: Avoid the default `0 2px 4px rgba(0,0,0,0.5)`. It looks "cheap tech." Stick to the Ambient Shadow spec in Section 4.
*   **Don't Overuse Gold**: If everything is "Gold," nothing is premium. Reserve it for the top 5% of your UI's importance.
